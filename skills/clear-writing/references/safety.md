# Safety Overlay

Apply these rules to text that controls a risk. Also apply the procedure rules
to the structure of a safety instruction.

## Rules 7.1–7.3

| Rule | Operational check | Official PDF pages |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Use an applicable word or symbol to identify the risk level. Base the selection on an accurate risk analysis. | 103–104 |
| 7.2 | Start a safety instruction with a clear, accurate command or a necessary condition. | 103, 105 |
| 7.3 | State the specific hazard or possible result when possible. | 103, 106 |

Use this sequence:

1. Identify the risk level with an applicable label or, when applicable, a
   symbol. Select it from an accurate risk analysis.
2. Start with a clear preventive command. If a prerequisite condition is
   necessary, put the condition before the command.
3. State the specific hazard or result of noncompliance when possible.

In aerospace and defense, `WARNING` identifies injury or death risk and
`CAUTION` identifies object-damage risk. Use `WARNING` when the two risks apply.
Other domains can use different labels or symbols. Apply Rules 7.1–7.3 to the
content. STE does not specify uppercase or other safety formatting. Use the
applicable risk analysis and publication directive.

Safety instructions principally belong to procedures. Descriptions normally
exclude them unless they quote a safety instruction or safety risk is itself
the subject.

Example when the risk analysis identifies an electrical-injury risk:

```text
WARNING: Disconnect the electrical power before you touch the bare wire.
Electrical current can cause injury or death.
```

Source: definitions and Rules 7.1–7.3, official PDF pages 103–106. Rule 7.1 is
on pages 103–104. Rule 7.2 is on pages 103 and 105. Rule 7.3 is on pages 103
and 106.
