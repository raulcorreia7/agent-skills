# Common Writing Rules

Apply these common rules to technical and formal prose.

## Multi-Word Nouns

| Rule | Operational check | Official PDF pages |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Keep an ordinary multi-word noun to three words or fewer. Unpack a longer group with a preposition or clause. | 63–64 |
| 2.2 | Write an authorized long technical noun in full at first use. Explain it if necessary, then use an approved abbreviation or a clear short term. | 63–66 |

Do not abbreviate an approved term of three words or fewer only to shorten it.
Do not use too many abbreviations in procedures. Preserve official hyphens.
Use hyphens only for directly related words. Do not fully hyphenate a group of
more than three words.

## Verbs

| Rule | Operational check | Official PDF pages |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Use only the forms listed for an approved verb. | 67–68 |
| 3.2 | Use only the infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, or an allowed adjectival past participle. | 67–68 |
| 3.3 | Use a listed past participle for condition only before a noun or after `be`, `become`, or `stay`. A separately approved adjective remains permitted. | 67–69 |
| 3.4 | Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions. | 67, 69 |
| 3.5 | Do not use an `-ing` verb form. Rule 3.5 permits an approved word with that spelling. It also permits a technical noun or a functional modifier in a technical noun. | 67, 70–71 |
| 3.6 | Use active voice. In descriptions, use passive voice only when the agent is unknown. | 67, 71–75 |
| 3.7 | Use an approved verb for an action instead of a noun or another part of speech. | 67, 75 |

Procedures normally use imperatives. Descriptions identify the real agent and
use a simple tense. `You`, `we`, or `something` can identify an agent only when
technically correct. Never invent an agent to remove passive voice.

## Sentences

| Rule | Operational check | Official PDF pages |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Write a short, concrete, accurate, unambiguous sentence. A procedure addresses the reader. A description has one subject and no imperative. | 77–79 |
| 4.2 | Keep necessary nouns, verbs, subjects, and articles. Write contractions in full. | 77, 79–80 |
| 4.3 | Use a vertical list for complex text with many items or actions. | 77, 80–83 |
| 4.4 | Connect related sentences with an approved connecting word, phrase, or demonstrative adjective. | 77, 83–84 |
| 4.5 | Use an article or demonstrative adjective when applicable. Omit an article for a general or abstract meaning. Also omit it before a noun immediately identified by an alphanumeric ID. | 77, 84–85 |

Before a vertical list, use a colon. Give each item an identifier and an
uppercase initial. Make each item grammatical with the lead-in. Keep one list
level, do not mix procedures and descriptions, and do not end items with
commas or semicolons. Write `DO NOT` in every safety item to which it applies.

## Automation Boundary

Software can flag contractions, likely verb forms, auxiliary stacks, and
phrasal-verb candidates. It cannot reliably determine sentence subject,
technical meaning, agent identity, or an approved dictionary sense. It cannot
determine whether an `-ing` word is a permitted technical noun. Treat these
semantic findings as `Review` items.
